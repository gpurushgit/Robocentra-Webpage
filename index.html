In the era of frontier AI, the security of robots matters most.

AI brings tremendous benefits, and it also makes breaches easier and more likely. The security of the robots that run your operations, in a plant, a warehouse or anywhere else, is paramount. They can be affected in many ways, individually or in combination.

Why robots need vacuum-tight security

When there is a breach, there are severe direct and indirect impacts.

A breached laptop leaks data. A breached robot can also act: change a speed limit, open a safety zone, drive into an aisle or stop a line. Those are the direct impacts. The indirect ones follow: lost orders, recovery costs, insurance and regulatory trouble, and damaged trust. An attacker needs only one gap, so a robot needs vacuum-tight security: no gaps at all.

Real OT and plant shop-floor breaches

Jul 2026 · Food plant

Fairlife halts all four US plants

Ransomware hit production-related systems at Coca-Cola's Fairlife dairy unit and stopped production at its four U.S. facilities for about 11 days. Dragos saw no direct control-system manipulation; the damage came through IT systems that OT depends on.

Read at Dragos
Apr 7, 2026 (updated Jul 22) · PLCs

Exposed PLCs exploited, HMI screens altered

A joint FBI, CISA and NSA advisory says Iran-affiliated attackers disrupted internet-exposed PLCs, starting with Rockwell Allen-Bradley CompactLogix and Micro850, and changed what operators saw on HMI screens. A July update added Schneider and Siemens.

Read at FBI, CISA and NSA advisory
May 2026 · Cobot controller

Universal Robots PolyScope 5 command injection

A critical flaw (CVE-2026-8153, CVSS 9.8) lets an unauthenticated attacker who can reach the Dashboard Server port run commands on the robot. The fix is in PolyScope 5.25.1.

Read at SecurityWeek
Mar 11, 2026 · Medical device plant

Stryker disruption reaches manufacturing and shipping

A global disruption in Stryker's Microsoft environment hit order processing, manufacturing and shipping. The Iran-linked group Handala claimed it, and a source reportedly said the attacker used a compromised admin account to send an Intune wipe command.

Read at Kaspersky ICS CERT
Feb 16 to Mar 30, 2026 · Food processing

Hazeldenes chicken site cannot package product

A cyberattack that DragonForce claimed left the Australian chicken processor unable to package products at one site, and some orders went unfilled. It returned to regular production on March 30.

Read at Kaspersky ICS CERT
Sep 2025 · Automotive

Jaguar Land Rover shuts UK plants

An attack shut Jaguar Land Rover's UK plants and halted production of about 1,000 vehicles a day. The company later said it would cut 4,000 jobs, blaming the attack.

Read at SecurityWeek
